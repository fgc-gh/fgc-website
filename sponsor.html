Fertile Ground Conservancy provides 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsorship to grassroots ecological initiatives. We are not a neutral platform — we sponsor projects aligned with biocentric values, including ecodefense work that mainstream conservation institutions won't touch. If your initiative is doing the right work and needs an organizational home, read on.

Who We Sponsor

We work with projects that are:

  • Biocentric in their values and approach
  • Grassroots and community-led
  • Working on ecological protection, habitat defense, Indigenous land rights, or related social justice issues
  • Operating in good faith with affected communities
  • Not duplicative of existing well-funded efforts

We have sponsored projects across the western United States and internationally, including efforts focused on mining resistance, urban permaculture, climate grief, Zen ecological practice, and rural agroforestry. Ecodefense initiatives — administrative advocacy, legal intervention, watchdog campaigns — are explicitly welcome and align directly with our own organizational practice.

What We Offer

Our administrative fee is 9% of funds received. Sponsored projects receive:

  • 501(c)(3) status — tax-deductible donations; eligibility for foundation grants
  • Financial administration — we receive, hold, and disburse funds with full accounting
  • Tax compliance — 990 inclusion; donor acknowledgment letters
  • Quarterly financial reporting — clear statements of income and expenditure
  • Grant writing support — drafting and reviewing grant applications
  • Donor communications support — appeals, acknowledgments, and updates
  • Organizational policy templates — conflict of interest, whistleblower, document retention
  • Annual report preparation — narrative and financial summary for your funders

Apply for Sponsorship

Before applying, read through our biocentric orientation to make sure there's genuine alignment. We review applications on a rolling basis and typically respond within three weeks.

We operate on the unceded territories of the Tolowa, Yurok, Karuk, and other Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Coast. These nations have lived in constitutive relationship with this land for thousands of years — not managing it, not owning it, but belonging to it in a way that shaped their language, governance, ceremony, and body of knowledge. What FGC is trying to practice, they have been practicing since long before conservation was a word. We are working to act in a manner consistent with that prior claim, and we fall short of it regularly. We name this not as ceremony but as accountability.
